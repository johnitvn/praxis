# Workflow: Design

Create solutions to problems through structured design reasoning.

## Purpose

Design is the process of creating something new to solve a problem. Unlike diagnosis (finding what's wrong) or decision (choosing among options), design creates options that didn't previously exist.

## When to Use

- You need to create something new, not just choose or fix
- The problem is ill-defined and the solution space is open
- Iteration and prototyping are feasible
- Users or stakeholders have needs that must be understood
- The solution must satisfy multiple competing constraints

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Understand

**Goal**: Understand the problem, context, and stakeholders.

1. **Who is this for?** Identify users and stakeholders
2. **What are their needs?** Not just what they say, but what they actually need
3. **What is the context?** Constraints, environment, existing systems
4. **What does success look like?** How will you know if the design is good?

**Methods**: Empathy mapping, stakeholder analysis, problem framing

### Phase 2: Define

**Goal**: Define the problem to be solved.

1. **Synthesize understanding**: What patterns emerged from the understand phase?
2. **State the problem**: A clear, actionable problem statement
3. **Identify constraints**: What must the solution satisfy?
4. **Define requirements**: What must the solution do?

**Methods**: Problem definition, constraint analysis, requirements elicitation

### Phase 3: Ideate

**Goal**: Generate a wide range of possible solutions.

1. **Generate quantity**: Aim for many ideas, not good ideas
2. **Suspend judgment**: Don't evaluate during ideation
3. **Build on others' ideas**: Combine, extend, remix
4. **Push past the obvious**: The first ideas are usually the most conventional

**Methods**: Divergent thinking, lateral thinking, brainstorming

### Phase 4: Prototype

**Goal**: Create low-fidelity versions of promising ideas.

1. **Select ideas to prototype**: The most promising and the most different
2. **Build cheap and fast**: Don't invest in polish at this stage
3. **Make it testable**: The prototype should answer a specific question
4. **Create multiple prototypes**: Parallel exploration

**Methods**: Prototyping, iterative design, experimentation

### Phase 5: Test

**Goal**: Test prototypes with real users or in realistic conditions.

1. **Define test objectives**: What are you trying to learn?
2. **Test with real users**: Not just the design team
3. **Observe, don't explain**: Watch what users do, not what they say
4. **Capture feedback systematically**

**Methods**: User testing, experimentation, observation

### Phase 6: Learn and Refine

**Goal**: Use test results to improve the design.

1. **What worked?** What should be kept?
2. **What didn't work?** What should be changed?
3. **What surprised you?** Unexpected findings are the most valuable
4. **Refine the design**: Incorporate learnings

**Methods**: Convergent thinking, evidence synthesis, iterative refinement

### Phase 7: Iterate

**Goal**: Repeat the prototype-test-refine cycle until the design is good enough.

1. **Is the design meeting requirements?**
2. **Are users satisfied?**
3. **Are there remaining issues?**
4. **Is further iteration warranted?** (cost vs. benefit)

**Methods**: Stopping criteria, iterative design

### Phase 8: Deliver

**Goal**: Finalize and communicate the design.

1. **Document the design**: Rationale, decisions, trade-offs
2. **Communicate to implementers**: What needs to be built?
3. **Define success metrics**: How will you measure success?
4. **Plan for iteration**: Designs evolve; plan for it

**Methods**: Documentation, communication, planning

## Common Failure Modes

- **Solution-first thinking**: Jumping to a solution before understanding the problem
- **Designing for yourself**: Assuming users are like you
- **Insufficient iteration**: Stopping at the first prototype
- **Not testing with real users**: Testing only with the design team
- **Over-polishing prototypes**: Investing too much in early prototypes
- **Feature creep**: Adding features without validating user need
