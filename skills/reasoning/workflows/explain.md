# Workflow: Explain

Produce clear, structured explanations of complex topics.

## Purpose

Explanation is the process of making something clear to an audience. It is not the same as listing facts — it's about building understanding in someone else's mind.

## When to Use

- You need to explain a complex concept or system
- Your audience has less domain knowledge than you
- The explanation needs to be clear and actionable
- You're teaching, documenting, or communicating reasoning

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Understand the Audience

**Goal**: Know who you're explaining to.

1. **What does the audience already know?**
2. **What do they need to know?**
3. **What are their likely misconceptions?**
4. **What is their goal?** (decide, understand, implement, evaluate)

**Methods**: Audience analysis, empathy, perspective-taking

### Phase 2: Identify the Core Message

**Goal**: Distill to the essential point.

1. **What is the one thing they should remember?**
2. **What is the answer, not the journey to the answer?**
3. **What would you say if you only had 30 seconds?**

**Methods**: Synthesis, prioritization, simplification

### Phase 3: Structure the Explanation

**Goal**: Organize for clarity and progression.

Choose a structure:

| Structure | When to Use |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Answer-first** | Audience wants the conclusion quickly |
| **Problem-solution** | Explaining a design or decision |
| **Simple-to-complex** | Building understanding incrementally |
| **Known-to-unknown** | Anchoring in familiar concepts |
| **Cause-effect** | Explaining why something happened |
| **Compare-contrast** | Explaining options or trade-offs |
| **Chronological** | Explaining a process or history |

**Methods**: Structure selection, organization, progression

### Phase 4: Build From Familiar Ground

**Goal**: Connect new ideas to known concepts.

1. **What does the audience already understand?**
2. **How can you connect new ideas to known ones?**
3. **What analogies or metaphors would help?**
4. **Start with what they know, build to what they don't**

**Methods**: Analogical reasoning, bridging, scaffolding

### Phase 5: Explain, Then Elaborate

**Goal**: Layer detail progressively.

1. **Start with the simple version**: The core idea
2. **Add detail**: Why, how, when, exceptions
3. **Add examples**: Concrete illustrations
4. **Add nuance**: Limitations, edge cases, context

**Methods**: Progressive elaboration, layered explanation

### Phase 6: Use Examples and Analogies

**Goal**: Make abstract concepts concrete.

1. **Provide at least one concrete example**
2. **Use analogies to familiar domains**
3. **Show, don't just tell**
4. **Use counterexamples**: What it's NOT, edge cases

**Methods**: Analogical reasoning, exemplification, concreteness

### Phase 7: Check for Understanding

**Goal**: Verify the explanation worked.

1. **Would the audience be able to apply this?**
2. **What questions would they ask?**
3. **What might they misunderstand?**
4. **Test the explanation**: Try explaining it to yourself

**Methods**: Verification, perspective-taking, testing

## Common Failure Modes

- **Curse of knowledge**: Assuming the audience knows what you know
- **Too much detail**: Overwhelming with information before establishing understanding
- **Too little detail**: Being too vague to be useful
- **Jargon without explanation**: Using technical terms the audience doesn't know
- **No examples**: Explaining abstractly without making it concrete
- **Wrong structure**: Using a structure that doesn't fit the audience's needs
- **Explaining the journey, not the destination**: Describing how you figured it out rather than the answer
