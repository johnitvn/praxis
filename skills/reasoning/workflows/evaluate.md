# Workflow: Evaluate

Systematically assess the quality, value, or correctness of something.

## Purpose

Evaluation is the process of judging merit, worth, or correctness against criteria. It is distinct from decision (which selects among options) and diagnosis (which finds causes). Evaluation answers "how good is this?"

## When to Use

- You need to assess the quality of a solution, design, or argument
- You need to compare something against standards or criteria
- You need to determine if something is fit for purpose
- You need to identify strengths and weaknesses
- A decision depends on knowing how good something is

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Define Purpose

**Goal**: Clarify what you're evaluating and why.

1. **What is being evaluated?** (solution, design, argument, plan, product)
2. **Why is it being evaluated?** (acceptance, improvement, comparison, auditing)
3. **Who is the evaluation for?** (yourself, stakeholders, users)
4. **What decisions depend on the evaluation?**

**Methods**: Problem framing, stakeholder analysis

### Phase 2: Establish Criteria

**Goal**: Define what "good" means for this evaluation.

1. **What are the relevant criteria?** (correctness, performance, usability, cost, etc.)
2. **What are the minimum acceptable standards?** (thresholds)
3. **What are the aspirational standards?** (excellence)
4. **Are criteria weighted?** If some matter more than others

**Methods**: Criteria definition, multi-criteria framework, standards identification

### Phase 3: Gather Evidence

**Goal**: Collect information relevant to the evaluation.

1. **What evidence is available?** (measurements, tests, reviews, feedback)
2. **What evidence is needed but missing?**
3. **How reliable is the evidence?**
4. **Gather the evidence**

**Methods**: Evidence gathering, measurement, testing, review

### Phase 4: Assess Against Criteria

**Goal**: Judge how well the subject meets each criterion.

1. **Score each criterion**: Based on the evidence
2. **Note uncertainty**: Where evidence is weak or missing
3. **Identify strengths**: Where does it exceed standards?
4. **Identify weaknesses**: Where does it fall short?

**Methods**: Evidence evaluation, scoring, gap analysis

### Phase 5: Identify Gaps

**Goal**: Find what's missing or insufficient.

1. **What criteria are not met?**
2. **How severe are the gaps?**
3. **Are gaps fixable?** At what cost?
4. **Are there hidden gaps?** Things not covered by your criteria?

**Methods**: Gap analysis, completeness check, risk assessment

### Phase 6: Synthesize Judgment

**Goal**: Form an overall assessment.

1. **Overall, how good is it?**
2. **What are the key strengths?**
3. **What are the critical weaknesses?**
4. **What is the confidence in this assessment?**

**Methods**: Synthesis, evidence synthesis, confidence calibration

### Phase 7: Recommend

**Goal**: Based on the evaluation, what should happen?

1. **Accept, reject, or revise?**
2. **What improvements are needed?**
3. **What are the priorities for improvement?**
4. **What follow-up evaluation is needed?**

**Methods**: Recommendation, prioritization, action planning

## Common Failure Modes

- **Missing criteria**: Evaluating without defining what "good" means
- **Halo effect**: Letting one positive quality color the entire evaluation
- **Horns effect**: Letting one negative quality color the entire evaluation
- **Recency bias**: Overweighting recent evidence
- **Confirmation bias**: Seeking evidence that confirms prior judgment
- **Criteria without evidence**: Scoring criteria based on assumptions, not evidence
- **False precision**: Giving numerical scores without meaningful measurement
