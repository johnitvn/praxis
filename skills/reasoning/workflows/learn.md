# Workflow: Learn

Systematically acquire new knowledge or skills.

## Purpose

Learning is the process of acquiring knowledge, understanding, or capability. This workflow structures learning for AI agents — both learning from interactions and learning from deliberate study.

## When to Use

- You need to understand a new domain, technology, or concept
- You need to develop a new capability
- You've encountered something you don't understand
- You need to stay current with changes
- The cost of not knowing exceeds the cost of learning

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Identify the Learning Goal

**Goal**: Define what you need to learn and why.

1. **What do you need to learn?** Be specific
2. **Why do you need to learn it?** The purpose drives the approach
3. **What level of understanding is needed?** Surface familiarity, working knowledge, or deep expertise?
4. **What is the deadline?** When do you need to know it by?

**Methods**: Goal definition, knowledge gap analysis

### Phase 2: Assess Current Knowledge

**Goal**: Understand what you already know.

1. **What do you already know about this topic?**
2. **What are your knowledge gaps?**
3. **What are your misconceptions?** (hardest to self-assess)
4. **What related knowledge do you have?** (transferable concepts)

**Methods**: Knowledge assessment, self-assessment, gap analysis

### Phase 3: Select Learning Strategy

**Goal**: Choose how to learn based on the goal and domain.

| Strategy | Best For | Approach |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Study** | Conceptual knowledge | Read, analyze, synthesize |
| **Practice** | Skills, procedures | Do, get feedback, refine |
| **Explore** | Novel domains | Survey, map, identify patterns |
| **Explain** | Deep understanding | Teach it, write about it |
| **Compare** | Nuanced understanding | Compare and contrast with known concepts |
| **Apply** | Practical knowledge | Use it to solve a real problem |

**Methods**: Learning strategy selection, metacognition

### Phase 4: Acquire

**Goal**: Gather information and experience.

1. **Find authoritative sources**: Documentation, papers, experts
2. **Study systematically**: Progressive, structured
3. **Take notes**: Not just consumption — processing
4. **Connect to existing knowledge**: How does this relate to what you know?

**Methods**: Research, study, information gathering, elaboration

### Phase 5: Practice and Apply

**Goal**: Move from knowing about to knowing how.

1. **Apply the knowledge**: Use it to solve a problem
2. **Test your understanding**: Try to explain it
3. **Identify gaps**: What don't you understand yet?
4. **Iterate**: Study more, apply again

**Methods**: Deliberate practice, application, testing

### Phase 6: Verify Understanding

**Goal**: Check whether you actually understand.

1. **Can you explain it simply?** (Feynman technique)
2. **Can you apply it to a novel problem?**
3. **Can you identify edge cases and limitations?**
4. **Can you teach it to someone else?**

**Methods**: Verification, self-testing, explanation, falsification

### Phase 7: Integrate

**Goal**: Connect new knowledge to existing knowledge.

1. **How does this relate to what you already knew?**
2. **What changed?** Did you correct misconceptions?
3. **What are the key mental models?** The core concepts
4. **What are the boundaries?** Where does this knowledge apply and not apply?

**Methods**: Synthesis, knowledge integration, mental model construction

### Phase 8: Maintain

**Goal**: Retain and update knowledge.

1. **What will you forget?** Plan for spaced review
2. **What will change?** Plan for updates
3. **What are the triggers for re-learning?** When should you refresh?

**Methods**: Spaced repetition, knowledge maintenance, updating

## Common Failure Modes

- **Passive consumption**: Reading/watching without processing
- **Illusion of competence**: Thinking you understand when you don't
- **Shallow learning**: Stopping at surface familiarity
- **Not applying**: Knowing about but not knowing how
- **Not verifying**: Assuming you understand correctly
- **Learning without purpose**: Learning things you don't need
- **Learning too broadly**: Not focusing on what matters for the goal
