# Self-Critique

Critically examine your own reasoning and conclusions.

## Purpose

Self-critique is the deliberate practice of finding flaws in your own reasoning before someone else does. It is the internal adversarial review.

## The Self-Critique Stance

Adopt the stance of a skeptical, intelligent reviewer who:
- Assumes the conclusion might be wrong
- Looks for the weakest link in the reasoning chain
- Does not accept "probably" without asking "how probably?"
- Is not impressed by confidence or conviction

## Procedure

### Step 1: The Inversion

Ask: "If my conclusion is wrong, what is the most likely reason?"

This is not "could it be wrong?" (the answer is almost always yes). It's "what is the MOST LIKELY reason it is wrong?"

### Step 2: Weakest Link Analysis

Examine each step in your reasoning chain:
- Which step has the weakest evidence?
- Which assumption is most uncertain?
- Which inference is least reliable?
- Which method's limitations are most relevant?

Focus critique on the weakest link — that's where errors are most likely.

### Step 3: Alternative Explanations

For each finding:
- What else could explain this?
- What would I expect to see if my conclusion were wrong?
- Have I considered the most plausible alternative?

### Step 4: Evidence Audit

For each piece of evidence:
- How reliable is the source?
- Could the evidence be interpreted differently?
- What evidence would contradict my conclusion?
- Have I searched for that evidence?

### Step 5: Method Critique

For each method used:
- Were its assumptions met?
- Were its limitations relevant?
- Would a different method produce a different result?
- If so, why should I trust this method over that one?

### Step 6: Severity Assessment

If the conclusion is wrong:
- How wrong is it? (Direction? Magnitude? Both?)
- What are the consequences?
- Is it wrong in a way that matters?

## Self-Critique Questions

### Evidence Questions
- What is the strongest evidence AGAINST my conclusion?
- What evidence would make me change my mind?
- Have I looked for disconfirming evidence?
- Am I treating weak evidence as strong because it supports my view?

### Reasoning Questions
- Where is my reasoning weakest?
- What step would I be least confident defending?
- If I had to bet against myself, which step would I bet fails?

### Conclusion Questions
- Could the opposite be true?
- Could a weaker version be true? A stronger version?
- What's the simplest explanation that fits the evidence?
- Am I overfitting to the specific evidence?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Performative critique**: Going through the motions without genuinely trying to find flaws
- **Surface critique**: Only critiquing minor points, not the core argument
- **Asymmetric critique**: Being much harder on conclusions you don't like
- **Critique paralysis**: Finding flaws in everything and being unable to conclude anything
- **Defensive response**: Treating critique as an attack rather than an improvement tool
