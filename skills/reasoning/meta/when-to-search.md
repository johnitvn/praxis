# When to Search

Decide when to search for information externally.

## Purpose

Searching is often the cheapest way to acquire information. But not everything worth knowing is worth searching for, and search results vary in quality.

## When to Search

### Search When:
- The information is likely to be publicly available
- The information is factual, not opinion-based
- The cost of search is low relative to the value of information
- You need domain-specific knowledge you don't have
- You need current information (recent events, latest versions)
- You need to verify a claim or fact
- You need examples, references, or documentation
- The user expects you to be informed before asking them

## When NOT to Search

### Don't Search When:
- The information is in the conversation context already
- The information is not material to the conclusion
- The search would take longer than reasoning with what you know
- The information is proprietary or internal (can't be found publicly)
- The information requires human judgment (ask instead)
- You're searching to avoid thinking (search procrastination)
- The information is about user preferences (ask, don't search)

## Search Strategy

### 1. Scope Before Searching
Define what you're looking for before you search. A clear question produces better results.

### 2. Evaluate Sources
Not all search results are equal:
- **Preferred**: Official documentation, primary sources, peer-reviewed research
- **Acceptable**: Reputable technical sources, established reference works
- **Caution**: Blog posts, forums, unverified claims
- **Avoid**: Clearly outdated, anonymous, or promotional content

### 3. Triangulate
For important facts, verify against multiple independent sources.

### 4. Stop Searching When:
- You've found a reliable answer
- Further searching is not improving the answer
- The cost of continued search exceeds the value of improvement
- You're seeing the same information repeated (saturation)

## Search Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Search procrastination** | Searching instead of thinking |
| **Search rabbit hole** | Following interesting but irrelevant results |
| **First-result satisfaction** | Accepting the first result without evaluation |
| **Source blindness** | Not evaluating the credibility of sources |
| **Confirmation search** | Only searching for what confirms your view |
| **Over-search** | Continuing to search after finding adequate information |
