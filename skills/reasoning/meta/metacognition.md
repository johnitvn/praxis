# Metacognition

Monitor and regulate your own reasoning.

## Purpose

Metacognition is the ability to observe, evaluate, and control your own cognitive processes. It is the foundation of all other meta-reasoning capabilities.

## The Metacognitive Loop

```
Plan → Monitor → Evaluate → Regulate → Plan → ...
```

### 1. Plan
Before reasoning, ask:
- What kind of problem is this?
- What method is appropriate?
- What depth is appropriate?
- What would a good answer look like?

### 2. Monitor
During reasoning, watch for:
- Is the method working?
- Are assumptions holding?
- Is progress being made?
- Am I converging or diverging?
- Is confidence increasing appropriately?

### 3. Evaluate
After each reasoning step:
- Is the result plausible?
- Is it consistent with what I know?
- Is the confidence level appropriate?
- Are there gaps or errors?

### 4. Regulate
If evaluation reveals issues:
- Adjust the method or depth
- Challenge assumptions
- Gather more information
- Switch perspective
- Escalate or stop

## Metacognitive Triggers

| Signal | Meaning | Action |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Reasoning feels too easy | May be oversimplifying | Check assumptions, increase depth |
| Reasoning feels stuck | May be in a mental rut | Switch perspective, try lateral thinking |
| Confidence is very high | May be overconfident | Check calibration, try falsification |
| Confidence is very low | May be missing information | Gather information, reduce uncertainty |
| Answer came too quickly | May be pattern-matching, not reasoning | Verify, check for anchoring |
| Answer is taking too long | May be overthinking | Check stopping criteria |
| Result is surprising | May be an error or a breakthrough | Verify carefully |
| Result is exactly as expected | May be confirmation bias | Try to disprove |

## Metacognitive Questions

Ask yourself periodically:

1. **What am I doing right now?** (not what should I be doing — what am I actually doing)
2. **Is this the right approach?** (given the problem, stakes, and constraints)
3. **What am I missing?** (unknowns, perspectives, alternatives)
4. **How would I know if I were wrong?** (falsification conditions)
5. **Should I continue?** (or change approach, or stop)

## Common Metacognitive Failures

| Failure | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Metacognitive blindness** | Not monitoring reasoning at all |
| **Metacognitive overconfidence** | Thinking you're reasoning well when you're not |
| **Metacognitive neglect** | Monitoring but not regulating |
| **Metacognitive rumination** | Over-monitoring, never acting |
| **Metacognitive illusion** | Feeling like you understand when you don't |

## Calibration

Good metacognition produces calibrated confidence:
- When you're 90% confident, you're right ~90% of the time
- When you're 50% confident, you're right ~50% of the time

If your confidence is consistently higher than your accuracy, your metacognition is failing.
