# Alternative Generation

Generate alternative hypotheses, explanations, solutions, and perspectives.

## Purpose

The quality of reasoning is bounded by the quality of the alternatives considered. If the right answer is not among the options you consider, you cannot reach it.

## When to Generate Alternatives

- **Before concluding**: Ensure you've considered a sufficient range of possibilities
- **When confidence is too high**: Overconfidence often comes from not considering alternatives
- **When stuck**: A new alternative can break an impasse
- **When verifying**: The best verification is comparing against the best alternative

## Alternative Types

### 1. Alternative Hypotheses
What else could explain the observations?
- **Null hypothesis**: Nothing interesting is happening (chance, noise)
- **Rival hypothesis**: A different causal mechanism
- **Reverse hypothesis**: The opposite causal direction
- **Broader hypothesis**: A more general phenomenon
- **Narrower hypothesis**: A more specific phenomenon

### 2. Alternative Solutions
What other ways could the problem be solved?
- **Different approach**: A fundamentally different method
- **Different scope**: Solve a bigger or smaller problem
- **Different constraints**: Relax or tighten constraints
- **Non-solution**: Is "do nothing" viable?

### 3. Alternative Perspectives
How would someone else see this?
- **Stakeholder views**: How would each affected party see it?
- **Expert views**: How would a domain expert see it?
- **Adversarial view**: How would a competitor or critic see it?
- **Naive view**: How would someone with no domain knowledge see it?

### 4. Alternative Futures
What else could happen?
- **Best case**: Everything goes right
- **Worst case**: Everything goes wrong
- **Most likely**: The central scenario
- **Wild card**: Low probability, high impact
- **Status quo**: Nothing changes

## Procedure

### Step 1: Generate (Quantity First)

Generate alternatives without evaluating them. Aim for at least 3-5 alternatives for any significant conclusion.

Techniques:
- **Inversion**: What's the opposite?
- **Extremes**: What are the extreme versions?
- **Random combination**: Combine elements in unexpected ways
- **Constraint removal**: What if a key constraint didn't exist?
- **Perspective shift**: How would [person X] see this?

### Step 2: Evaluate (Quality Second)

For each alternative:
- How plausible is it?
- What evidence supports it?
- What evidence would distinguish it from the others?
- What would be the implications if it were true?

### Step 3: Compare

Compare alternatives against each other:
- Which best explains the evidence?
- Which is simplest?
- Which has the fewest assumptions?
- Which makes the most testable predictions?

## Common Failure Modes

- **False dichotomies**: Only considering two alternatives
- **Straw man alternatives**: Generating weak alternatives to make your preferred one look better
- **Missing the best alternative**: Failing to generate the actually correct alternative
- **Premature evaluation**: Judging alternatives during generation, suppressing creativity
- **Alternative overload**: Generating too many alternatives and being unable to evaluate them
