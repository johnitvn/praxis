# Perspective Switching

Deliberately view the problem from different vantage points.

## Purpose

Every perspective is partial. Switching perspectives reveals blind spots, challenges assumptions, and produces more robust reasoning.

## Perspective Types

### 1. Disciplinary Perspectives

| Perspective | Asks |
|-------------|------|
| Engineer | How does it work? What could break? |
| Designer | How does it feel? What need does it serve? |
| Economist | What are the incentives? What are the trade-offs? |
| Strategist | Who wins? What are the competitive dynamics? |
| Scientist | What's the evidence? What's the mechanism? |
| Mathematician | What's provable? What follows necessarily? |
| Lawyer | What's permitted? What's the precedent? |
| Ethicist | What's right? Who is harmed? |
| User | What do I need? What frustrates me? |
| Operator | Can I run it? What happens when it fails? |
| Attacker | How can I break it? What's exposed? |
| Manager | What does it cost? When will it be done? |

### 2. Temporal Perspectives

| Perspective | Asks |
|-------------|------|
| Past | How did we get here? What has been tried? |
| Present | What is the current state? What's urgent? |
| Near future | What happens next? What are the immediate consequences? |
| Far future | What are the long-term effects? What will be different? |
| Hindsight | Looking back from the future, what will seem obvious? |

### 3. Stakeholder Perspectives

For each stakeholder:
- What do they care about?
- What do they know that I don't?
- What would they want me to consider?
- What would they object to?

### 4. Level Perspectives

| Level | Asks |
|-------|------|
| Detail | What are the specifics? What's the exact mechanism? |
| System | How does this fit into the larger system? |
| Meta | How am I thinking about this? What assumptions am I making? |

## Procedure

### Step 1: Identify Default Perspective

What perspective are you currently using? What does it emphasize? What does it miss?

### Step 2: Select Contrasting Perspectives

Pick 2-3 perspectives that are:
- Different from your default
- Relevant to the problem
- Likely to reveal something new

### Step 3: Switch Fully

For each perspective, genuinely adopt it. Don't just visit — inhabit it. Ask:
- What would I see that I'm not seeing now?
- What would I consider important that I'm dismissing?
- What would I question that I'm taking for granted?

### Step 4: Integrate

After switching:
- What did each perspective reveal?
- What contradictions exist between perspectives?
- What is more robust after considering multiple perspectives?
- What should change in your reasoning?

## When to Use

- **Before finalizing**: At minimum, check the "adversary" perspective
- **When consensus is too easy**: If everyone agrees, switch perspectives
- **When stuck**: A new perspective often reveals new paths
- **When stakes are high**: Multiple perspectives reduce blind spots
- **When stakeholders disagree**: Understand each perspective before resolving

## Common Failure Modes

- **Superficial switching**: Naming a perspective without genuinely adopting it
- **Confirmation switching**: Only switching to perspectives that confirm your view
- **Perspective hopping**: Switching so much you never settle on a conclusion
- **Caricature perspectives**: Using straw-man versions of other perspectives
