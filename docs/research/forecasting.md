# Forecasting — Research Notes

## Canonical Sources

- **Tetlock, P.E. & Gardner, D.** (Superforecasting, 2015) — IARPA tournament, superforecaster characteristics
- **Kahneman, D.** (Thinking, Fast and Slow, 2011) — Inside view vs. outside view, planning fallacy
- **Armstrong, J.S.** (Principles of Forecasting, 2001) — Reference class forecasting, combining forecasts
- **Silver, N.** (The Signal and the Noise, 2012) — Calibration, Bayesian updating for predictions
- **Mellers, B. et al.** (2014, 2015) — Psychological characteristics of superforecasters
- **Taleb, N.N.** (The Black Swan, 2007) — Fat tails, predictability limits, unknown unknowns

## Key Findings

### Reference Class Forecasting
- Kahneman's key insight: the "inside view" (focusing on the specific case) produces overoptimistic forecasts
- The "outside view" (anchoring in a reference class of similar cases) is more accurate
- Procedure: (1) identify reference class, (2) obtain distribution of outcomes, (3) adjust for case-specific differences
- The planning fallacy: people systematically underestimate time and cost
- Most common error: "this case is different" — it usually isn't

### Calibration
- A forecaster is calibrated if X% of their X%-confidence predictions come true
- Most people are overconfident: their 90% confidence intervals contain the truth ~50% of the time
- Brier score: mean squared error of probabilistic predictions
- Calibration ≠ discrimination: you can be well-calibrated but uninformative (always predicting base rate)
- Improving calibration: track predictions, get rapid feedback, decompose problems

### Ensemble Forecasting
- The "wisdom of crowds": averaged forecasts outperform individual forecasts
- Condition: errors must be uncorrelated (diverse perspectives)
- Simple mean often outperforms weighted mean (weights are noisy)
- "Extremizing": adjusting ensemble toward the extremes (counteracts regression to the mean)
- Danger: pseudo-diversity — multiple forecasters using the same source/ model

### Superforecasting
- Tetlock's IARPA tournament findings: some people consistently outperform others
- Superforecaster traits: cognitive (probabilistic thinking, numeracy), not ideological
- Key practices: Fermi estimation, Bayesian updating, outside view, decomposition
- Superforecasters update frequently (not stubbornly), in small increments
- The Brier score of top superforecasters is ~0.10 (vs. ~0.25 for chance)
- Most important skill: knowing what you don't know and quantifying it

## Method Boundaries

- **Forecasting vs. Planning**: Forecasting predicts what will happen; planning achieves what you want to happen. Both are needed for good strategy.
- **Reference Class vs. Bayesian**: Reference class provides the outside view (base rate); Bayesian reasoning updates with case-specific evidence. Combine them.
- **Calibration vs. Accuracy**: You can be well-calibrated but inaccurate (e.g., always predicting 50%). Calibration is about confidence alignment; accuracy is about being right.
