# Systems Thinking — Research Notes

## Canonical Sources

- **Meadows, D.H.** (Thinking in Systems, 2008) — Leverage points, feedback loops, system dynamics
- **Forrester, J.W.** (Industrial Dynamics, 1961) — System dynamics, stock and flow
- **Senge, P.M.** (The Fifth Discipline, 1990) — Learning organizations, systems archetypes
- **Holland, J.H.** (Complexity, 2014) — Complex adaptive systems, emergence
- **Barabási, A.L.** (Linked, 2002) — Network science, scale-free networks
- **Sterman, J.D.** (Business Dynamics, 2000) — System dynamics modeling

## Key Findings

### Systems Thinking
- Meadows' hierarchy of leverage points (from least to most effective):
  1. Constants, parameters, numbers
  2. Buffer sizes
  3. Stock-and-flow structure
  4. Delays
  5. Balancing feedback loops
  6. Reinforcing feedback loops
  7. Information flows
  8. Rules of the system
  9. Power to add/change system structure
  10. Goals of the system
  11. Mindset/paradigm out of which the system arises
  12. Power to transcend paradigms
- The most effective leverage points are also the hardest to change
- System boundaries are subjective and consequential — what's inside vs. outside determines what you see

### Feedback Analysis
- Reinforcing (positive) loops: growth, collapse, amplification (R)
- Balancing (negative) loops: stability, goal-seeking, oscillation (B)
- Delays in feedback loops cause oscillation and overshoot
- System archetypes: limits to growth, shifting the burden, tragedy of the commons, escalation
- Most common error: looking for linear causes in nonlinear systems

### Network Analysis
- Centrality measures: degree, betweenness, closeness, eigenvector
- Small-world networks: high clustering + short path lengths
- Scale-free networks: power-law degree distribution, hubs
- Community detection: modularity, hierarchical clustering
- Key insight: network structure determines system behavior (cascades, resilience, influence)

### Emergence Analysis
- Emergence: the whole has properties the parts don't
- Simple micro-rules can produce complex macro-behavior
- Cannot be predicted from parts alone; requires simulation or observation
- Self-organization: order emerges without central control
- AI agents commonly fail to recognize emergent behavior, attributing it to design or intent

## Method Boundaries

- **Systems Thinking vs. Causal Reasoning**: Both deal with causal relationships. Systems thinking emphasizes feedback loops and emergence; causal reasoning emphasizes interventions and counterfactuals.
- **Networks vs. Hierarchies**: Networks have no single root; hierarchies do. Many systems are hierarchies that behave like networks.
- **Emergence vs. Composition**: Composition is additive (whole = sum of parts); emergence is non-additive (whole ≠ sum of parts).
